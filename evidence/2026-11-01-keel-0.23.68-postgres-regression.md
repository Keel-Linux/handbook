## Part 1: how the writes were counted

The method differs from the MariaDB test in one point. keel's `pg_hba` permits
only local clients and the pair. So the writers ran on B and C on the local
socket, only while the node had the VIP. This is the method of
`evidence/2026-10-18-postgresql-pg-rewind.md`. No client went through the VIP
from W. An id counts as acknowledged only when `psql` returned that id within
15 s. After the rounds, the acknowledged ids of both writers were looked up on
both nodes.

## Part 1: pg_rewind

The 12 rounds above did not run `pg_rewind`: keel found the reseed cheaper. To
make a rewind more probable, the test loaded a table of 10000 rows into the
database (107 MB). The load took 2125 s through the synchronous commit.

| Round | Holder | Other writable | Heal to ready | Rejoin | Missing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| W1 | B | 32.7 s | 76 s | reseed: rewind 124 MB, 1355 s at 94 kB/s; reseed 36 MB | 0 |
| W2 | C | 27.6 s | 1223 s | `pg_rewind`, 1176.0 s | 0 |

So one `pg_rewind` on keel 0.23.68 passed, from B to C. While it ran, the new
primary committed with no standby for about 20 minutes. A loss of that node
in that time loses data. This is the cost that
`evidence/2026-10-18-postgresql-pg-rewind.md` recorded.

The test did not find why W2 chose the rewind and W1 the reseed. The journal
lines of W2 do not say it.

## Part 1: partition

An `nft` table in the CT dropped UDP 51820 in both directions for about 150 s.
The test sampled the VIP and `pg_is_in_recovery()` on both nodes, one node at a
time, about every 5 s.

Partition of the holder B. Times are seconds after the start of the step:

| Time | B | C |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 to 13 | has the VIP, writable | no VIP, standby |
| 18 to 19 | no VIP, not in recovery | no VIP, standby |
| 24 | no VIP, not in recovery | has the VIP, standby |
| 29 to 42 | no VIP, not in recovery | has the VIP, writable |
| 47 and later | server stopped by keel | has the VIP, writable |

- No sample showed the VIP on both nodes. The samples are 5 s apart, so this
  does not prove that no overlap shorter than 5 s occurred.
- Between 29 s and 42 s both servers were out of recovery. B was not a
  split brain for acknowledged writes: its last acknowledged write was at
  +2.0 s, because each commit waited for a standby that it could not reach.
- A commit that waits on B is visible to a local reader on B in that time.
  The test did not measure this.
- After the heal, the pair was ready in 58 s, with a reseed. 0 missing.

Partition of the replica B, holder C: the holder kept the VIP. Its commits
stopped for 161 s and continued after the heal, as the synchronous commit
requires (no fallback). `keel database watch` said "synchronous commit waiting"
after about 30 s. The pair was ready 32 s after the heal. 0 missing.

## What the test found, for the maintainer

None of these lost an acknowledged write.

1. **Reseeds keep data directories for up to 7 days.** After this test B has 9
   `main.keel-kept-*` directories and C has 8. On B they use about 1.4 GB, 18 %
   of the 8 GB disk. This is the documented rule of
   `docs/replication-postgresql.md`: keel keeps the newest, deletes the others
   after 7 days, and deletes the oldest first when free space is low. The test
   did not run long enough, or fill the disk enough, to see this removal. A dump
   for each rejoin is also kept (97 MB on each node).
2. **A rejoin on the poor link can leave the primary alone for 20 minutes** (W2).
3. **A wrong word in a log line.** On PostgreSQL the VIP message says "a MariaDB
   server here acknowledges no write".
4. **The first apply of a replica reports a failure** when the node has its own
   cluster, which is every node from this image. The message gives the next step.

## What went wrong in the test

- The first `mk.sh` read the passwords from the same standard input as the
  script, so no CT was made. The script was corrected.
- The first `create database` printed nothing, and the check ran too early. The
  writers logged errors until the database was there.
- The script's value "both writable samples 0" is wrong: the nodes are sampled
  at different seconds, so that count cannot be above 0. The table above comes
  from the raw samples, and it replaces that value.
- The script's `refused` count matched "connection refused" lines. It is not
  used here.

## What this test does not show

- A live pair that goes from 0.23.62 to 0.23.68 was not tested. This test and
  the MariaDB test upgraded before the pair existed.
- keel 0.23.68 does not have the fixes of the open PRs, or keel#110.
- No application ran on the pair. No client used the VIP.
- BR3-THIN was not used.
- The 12 small rounds used reseed. Only 1 `pg_rewind` ran on real nodes here.
