## The stall of 2 s

The condition did not change since keel 0.23.46: the drain ends by stall only
when neither the replayed nor the received position moved for the whole
stall. The coordinator asked for a proof that a stall can never end the drain
while replay moves. The tests are in `tests/test_system_dbpgpromote.py`,
`TestTheStallNeverEndsWhileReplayMoves`, and each runs with the limit of 5 s
and with the limit of 2 s:

| Test | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| a received position that stands still, replay that moves at every poll, 200 polls | the drain does not end; it times out after 150 s |
| replay that moves once in 2 polls, so it is quiet for 1 s at most | the drain does not end in 120 s |
| replay that stands still for the whole stall | the drain ends after exactly the limit |
| 300 seeded random runs of positions per limit | whenever the drain ends with replay behind the received position, the last window of the limit has one single pair of positions, and the run lasted at least the limit |

A mutation of the code, which counts the stall on the received position alone
(the case that the condition must not allow), makes 8 of these tests fail, so
the tests do see the fault. The module says it too: any step of the replayed
position, which is local, or of the received one starts the stall again.

What the stall can still end: a replay that is blocked for 2 s with whole
records pending (for example a query on the standby that holds it). That
promotion loses nothing, because `pg_promote()` replays every whole record
that is in `pg_wal` before it ends recovery. The coordinator asked about
this case and the changelog of 0.23.48 says it.

Try 3 on keel 0.23.48:

```
+2 s .. +19 s   t|0/5600000|0/55FFC08    (received|replayed, in recovery)
+25 s           f|...                    promoted
follow: 1016 bytes after the last record that replay applied were received and
```

With the cut at the same point of the test, the case without a partial record
promotes at +20.7 s to +21.9 s, with the 5 s stall at +28.8 s, and with

(2 s and up to 1 s of the poll) when the stream ended inside a record, and
nothing otherwise. In the failover round of this test the positions were equal
(no partial record), the first database write came 1.67 s after the VIP and
the Odoo write 4.41 s.

## The hang

The question: can the 15 minutes of the 19.0-8 bootstrap come back?

Facts (all in keel#219): the second node of a pair was seeding; `systemctl
restart postgresql` (the restart of `dbpgpairplan`) and `systemctl stop
postgresql@17-main` (the stop of `dbpgfollow._unproven`) ran at the same
second; the unit stayed `deactivating (final-sigterm)`, with `ExecStart`
killed by TERM and with only the postmaster, the checkpointer and the writer
left; `postgresql@.service` has `TimeoutStopSec=1h`; `kill -9` ended it.

The race between the 2 keel commands is real: apply restarts the unit while
follow, which a path unit starts when the role file changes, stops it, on a
node that has no proven claim, which is the first apply of every pair. A stop
that arrives while `ExecStart` runs does not run `ExecStop`: systemd sends
SIGTERM to the cgroup, which is a smart shutdown of the postmaster.

I could not make it hang again: 6 stops during a start of the replica (0.3 s
to 4.5 s), 6 reseeds with a stop at 0.5 s to 10 s, 7 restarts of the meta unit
against a stop on the holder, 7 on a standalone primary, and 2 more bootstraps
of a pair, all ended in under 0.2 s or normally. So it can come back, at a rate
of about 1 in 7 bootstraps so far, and a bootstrap then waits up to 1 hour with
no error. I did not ship `KillSignal=SIGINT` (the cheapest fix, in keel#219)
because I could not test it against the fault.
