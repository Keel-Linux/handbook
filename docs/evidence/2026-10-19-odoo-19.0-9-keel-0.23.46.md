## Uploads under netem

Attachments are in the database, and a commit waits for the synchronous
standby (decision 0054). With netem on the holder, the WAL crosses a link of
250 ms and 1.5 % loss, and a commit runs at about 35 KB/s. Two timeouts cut an
upload before it ended:

| Image | Upload of 4 MiB under netem | Cause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 19.0-7 | 504 after 63 s | the front waits 60 s for Anubis (the default of `proxy_read_timeout`) |
| 19.0-8 | 413 after 123 s | Odoo kills a worker at `limit_time_real` (120 s): `WorkerHTTP timeout after 120s` in the journal, then Nginx logs `upstream prematurely closed` and answers 413 |
| 19.0-9 | 200 after 126 s | both limits are 720 s |

Two more things of the slow link, which are not defects of the layer: the
first request after a large upload can wait minutes, because the commit of a
login (the session and its date) queues behind the WAL of the upload (one
login of the first try timed out after 60 s of the test client, and the
client now has a time limit of 300 s); and the same queue made the first
database write on the holder in failover round 1 wait 45 s before the cut (the
writer on C had no acknowledged write in that round).

## keel#215

The case: netem on the holder (250 ms ±25 ms, 1.5 % loss), a cut of the
holder, and the standby takes the VIP. On keel 0.23.45 the follow of the
standby polled the drain of the promotion for 417 s.

**Root cause.** `keel.system.dbpgpromote` waits until the replayed position
reaches the received one (`pg_last_wal_replay_lsn() >= pg_last_wal_receive_lsn()`).
The walreceiver flushes the bytes that arrive, and a cut can end the stream
inside a record. Replay cannot apply a part of a record, so it stands at the end
of the last whole record, and it never reaches the received position. The
two positions are still, and the drain cannot end on equality. The first
sighting was `0/7EC8000` received and `0/7EC7A58` replayed (1448 bytes, one TCP
segment). `pg_promote()` replays every whole record that is in `pg_wal`
before it ends recovery, so only the bytes of the unfinished record are left
behind, which no standby can apply.

**Reproduction on keel 0.23.45**, with a writer of big rows (320 KB, no
compression) on the holder, so that a record is in flight at the cut:

```
cut of the holder at +0 s, netem on the holder
+2 s .. +154 s   t|0/ACC0000|0/ACBFD60   follow=activating   (received|replayed, 672 bytes apart)
killed at +163 s, the standby still in recovery
```

The same case with no writer promotes in 22 s (the positions are equal at the
cut), so the case needs a record in flight.

**Fix (keel 0.23.46).** The drain also ends when neither position has moved
for 5 s (`STALL_SECONDS`). A standby whose positions still move keeps its
wait of 600 s. A standby that receives nothing is still refused. The follow
says how many bytes it left behind. 7 tests were written first (6 for the
function, 1 for the follow); the CI of the pull request ran the 11 jobs,
including the real PostgreSQL pair.

**Proof on the real pair, keel 0.23.46**, the same case:

```
+3 s .. +28 s    t|0/850A000|0/8509B68    (1176 bytes apart)
+35 s            f|...                    promoted
follow: 1176 bytes after the last record that replay applied were received and
        cannot be applied: an incomplete record, because the stream ended
        inside it. Neither position moved for 5 s, so the drain is over;
        pg_promote() replays every whole record that is in pg_wal first
Odoo serving on the new holder 28.8 s after the cut
```

| Case | keel | Standby after the cut | Promoted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| big-row writer, netem on the holder | 0.23.45 | 672 bytes apart, still | never in 163 s (killed) |
| big-row writer, netem on the holder | 0.23.46 | 1176 bytes apart, still | +28.8 s after the cut |
| no writer, netem on the holder | 0.23.46 | equal | +22.0 s after the cut |
| failover 1 of this test, writers, netem | 0.23.46 | 432 bytes apart, still | first DB write 6.79 s after the VIP |

The fix costs the 5 s of the stall when a cut ends the stream inside a record,
and nothing otherwise. Nothing acknowledged was lost: failover 1 had 897
acknowledged ids and 387 Odoo writes and none is missing on the survivor.

## The test

BR1 is hw12-br1, BR2 is nfs1-br2. BR3-THIN is offline, so the test cuts or
stops the CT of the holder and never the whole BR1 site.

| | BR1, witness | BR1, pair node | BR2, pair node |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Odoo | 9144 `odoo-w` | 9148 `odoo-b`, replica at installation | 9149 `odoo-c`, primary at installation |

Odoo writer through the VIP, each round with its own range of ids). The
database of each pair was made from the image of the version under test.

## Open

- **The 5 s of the stall.** A cut that ends the stream inside a record costs
  5 s before the promotion (round 1: the first database write 6.79 s after
  the VIP, and the Odoo write 9.21 s). A stall of 2 s would be enough on the
  measured cases (the cut came 20 s before the standby took the VIP, so the
  stream was dead long before), and it is not tried here.
- **A large upload keeps the pair busy.** After an upload of 10 MiB under
  netem the next commit waits for the WAL to cross the link.
- **The PostgreSQL server of a new pair stopped for 15 minutes at the
  bootstrap of the 19.0-8 test.** `systemctl stop` came while a start of the
  standby was running; the postmaster stayed in `epoll_wait` with only the
  checkpointer and the background writer, and I ended it with `kill -9` on the
  3 processes. It happened once in the 5 bootstraps of this work and is not analyzed.
- Test CTs left running: 9144 (BR1, witness), 9148 (BR1) and 9149 (BR2).
